# Relationship research for the five seasons

Research brief for the season spec. Researched September 2026. Each item has the finding,
what it means for a feature, and a source. **Evidence** notes say how solid it is. Where the
only sources were therapist blogs or vendor pages, this doc says so.

Season map used throughout: 1 Infatuation, 2 Power struggle, 3 Stability, 4 Commitment,
5 Co-creation.

---

## 1. Developmental stage models (Bader & Pearson; Campbell)

- **Bader & Pearson (1988), *In Quest of the Mythical Mate*.** The model draws on Mahler's child
  development stages and Bowen's idea of differentiation. Its stages are: symbiosis/bonding
  (merging, similarities), differentiation (differences show up and conflict has to be learned),
  practicing (each person turns back to their own friends and goals), rapprochement (moving
  apart and back together), and in some versions synergy.
  [Couples Institute](https://www.couplesinstitute.com/a-developmental-model-for-healthy-couples/)
- **Campbell (1980), *The Couple's Journey*.** This model was built from interviews with about
  100 couples. Its stages are Romance, Power Struggle, Stability, Commitment, Co-creation. Power
  struggle starts with "you're not who I thought you were". In co-creation the couple faces
  outward together (children, a project, community).
  [susancampbell.com](https://susancampbell.com/product/the-couples-journey-intimacy-as-a-path-to-wholeness-2/)
- **What derails couples at each stage:** idealising and missing red flags (1); treating
  differences as proof the match is wrong (2); drifting into parallel lives (3); taking the other
  for granted (4); letting the project replace the romance (5).
- **Evidence: weak to moderate.** Both are clinical frameworks, not tested stage theories. The
  longitudinal data complicates them. Lavner & Bradbury (2010) followed 464 newlyweds for 4 years
  and found that most couples were stable or only declined a little. The "honeymoon drop" came
  mostly from a small group who started low and fell fast.
  [Lavner & Bradbury 2010, *J Marriage & Family*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1741-3737.2010.00757.x)
- **Implication:** Treat stages as themes, not a ladder. Let couples pick any season and move
  between them. Never say "you are behind" or diagnose a stage (no scores, no guilt). Using
  stages to frame each season's content is fine. Claiming they are a science is not.

## 2. Gottman: the most useful findings

**Evidence caveat:** Much of the Gottman material is from Gottman's own lab and books.
Critics note that the "90% divorce prediction" figures came from fitting models after the fact
without cross-validation (Heyman & Slep, 2001, *J Marriage & Family*). The individual
behaviours are still well supported.

- **Love maps.** Knowing your partner's inner world (worries, hopes, history, preferences).
  In the Bringing Baby Home work (Shapiro, Gottman & Carrère, 2000), about two-thirds of couples
  had a sharp drop in satisfaction after their first baby. The ones who didn't knew each other's
  world in detail. [Gottman: Build Love Maps](https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-sound-relationship-house-build-love-maps/)
  *Implication:* "Guess my answer" cards (predict, then reveal) are exactly love-map building
  and fit a one-tap format. Include them in every season, and in Season 4 especially
  (taking each other for granted).
- **Bids and turning toward.** Newlyweds still married 6 years later had turned toward bids
  86% of the time in the lab. Those who divorced had done so 33% of the time.
  [Gottman: Emotional Bids](https://www.gottman.com/blog/an-introduction-to-emotional-bids-and-trust/)
  *Implication:* Add a tiny "bid" mechanic. One tap sends a small "saw this, thought of you"
  and one tap turns toward it (a reaction), with no reply required. Never show a
  "response rate". That would turn a research finding into a scorecard.
- **The 5:1 "magic ratio".** In stable couples, **during conflict**, there are at least 5
  positive interactions (interest, humour, affection, validation) for every negative one.
  Unhappy couples are near 1:1. [Gottman: Magic Ratio](https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-magic-relationship-ratio-according-science/)
  *Implication:* The ratio is about conflict. It is not a daily quota. Build it into the
  structure: any Season 2 card that touches a friction point is surrounded by warm or playful
  cards. Never count or show the ratio.
- **Four Horsemen and their antidotes.** Criticism is countered by a gentle start-up
  ("I feel … about … I need …"). Contempt by a culture of appreciation. Defensiveness by taking
  some responsibility. Stonewalling by self-soothing.
  [Gottman: Antidotes](https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-four-horsemen-the-antidotes/)
  *Implication:* Season 2 can offer "rewrite this gently" cards with playful, invented examples
  and never the couple's own words. Contempt (mockery, eye-rolling) is the strongest predictor
  of breakup, so the product's own voice must never mock either partner.
- **Repair attempts.** Anything that stops negativity from escalating: a joke, "can we start
  over?", a touch. *Implication:* A small, always-available "phir se? / let's start over"
  button or sticker is cheap and matches the research.
- **Flooding and the 20-minute break.** When heart rate spikes, listening fails. Take a break
  of at least 20 minutes and at most 24 hours, and spend it doing something distracting, not
  ruminating. [Gottman: Flooding](https://www.gottman.com/blog/does-flooding-play-a-role-in-your-perpetual-conflict/)
  *Implication:* Add a "pause" option on hard cards that holds the card for 20+ minutes and
  suggests a distraction. Use timestamp timers, which the browser rules already require. Pausing
  should say "I'll come back", not "I'm leaving".
- **Perpetual problems.** About 69% of conflict is about lasting differences in personality or
  lifestyle. The aim is ongoing dialogue, not a solution.
  [Gottman: Solvable vs Perpetual](https://www.gottman.com/blog/managing-conflict-solvable-vs-perpetual-problems/)
  *Implication:* This is the core message for Season 2: differences are normal and "we don't
  have to solve it today". It works directly against the season's risk (breaking up instead of
  growing).
- **State of the Union meeting.** A weekly check-in: appreciations, then stress-reducing talk,
  then one issue handled gently, then dreams.
  [Gottman: State of the Union](https://www.gottman.com/blog/how-to-have-a-state-of-the-union-meeting/)
  *Implication:* A 60-minute meeting breaks the 20-second rule. A **5-card mini version** does
  not: one appreciation, one stress, one small wish, one dream. It suits Seasons 3 to 5.
  Keep it optional so it never feels like a weekly obligation.
- **Small things often.** Gottman's central message: frequent small positive moments matter
  more than rare big gestures. This is exactly our one-tap format. Aim for short sessions
  rather than long ones.

## 3. Pursue–withdraw (demand–withdraw)

- **Finding:** When one partner pushes for change or engagement and the other avoids it, the
  pattern is reliably linked to lower satisfaction and intimacy. A meta-analysis of 74 studies
  (N = 14,255) found r ≈ .36 with poor outcomes. Either partner can take either role, depending
  on whose issue it is.
  [Schrodt, Witt & Shimkowski 2014, *Communication Monographs*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03637751.2013.813632)
- **EFT view (Sue Johnson):** Pursuing is attachment protest ("are you there for me?").
  Withdrawing is self-protection against feeling a failure or being overwhelmed. EFT effect
  sizes are large (d ≈ 1.3), though most trials are small and many are run by the model's
  developers. [Wiebe & Johnson 2016, *Family Process* (PDF)](https://drrebeccajorgensen.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Review-of-EFT-Research_Wiebe-and-Johnson..pdf)
- **IBCT (Christensen):** Aims at acceptance of differences as well as change. It holds gains
  better at 2 years than traditional behavioural therapy. About half of couples keep their gains
  at 5 years. [Christensen et al. 2010, *JCCP*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20350033/)
- **What helps each role:** Pursuers do better with a softer, time-limited ask ("5 minutes
  tonight?") and with reassurance that it will be addressed. Withdrawers do better with lower
  pressure, time to think, and a clear end point.
- **Implication:** The asynchronous "answer, then reveal" format already suits withdrawers
  because there is no live confrontation. For Season 2: let either person choose "later today"
  without penalty. Allow short or written answers. Avoid nagging notifications, since
  app-driven pursuit makes the pattern worse. Offer "what I do when I feel far from you" cards
  so each person can name their own move, with no labels like "you are the pursuer".

## 4. Nonviolent Communication (Rosenberg)

- **Finding:** NVC's four steps are: observation (no judgement), feeling, need, request (not
  demand). It is close to Gottman's gentle start-up.
- **Evidence: weak.** Most studies are small, uncontrolled, or not about couples (for example,
  parolees: Marlow et al. 2012). There are a few small couple-workshop studies, mostly in Iran,
  that report better marital adjustment.
  [CNVC research list](https://www.cnvc.org/learn/research) ·
  [NVC and parolees, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7727724/) ·
  [NVC marital adjustment workshop](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340542265_Effectiveness_of_nonviolent_communication_NVC_workshop_on_marital_adjustment)
- **Implication:** A "saying it softly" card template (I noticed / I felt / I need / would
  you…) is a useful one-line scaffold, and it fits "one line". Credit Gottman's better-evidenced
  gentle start-up as the main basis. Don't market the feature as NVC or promise results.

## 5. Aron: self-expansion and the 36 questions

- **Shared novel and arousing activities:** A newspaper survey, a door-to-door survey and 3 lab
  experiments all found the same thing. Doing a 7-minute novel, mildly arousing task together
  raised relationship quality more than a mundane task. The survey link between exciting
  activities and satisfaction ran through reduced boredom.
  [Aron et al. 2000, *JPSP*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10707334/)
  *Implication:* This is the evidence base for Season 3 (roommate drift) and Season 5 (keeping
  romance). Use "try something new this week" dares, small mini-challenges, and "plan a first
  time" cards. The novelty is what matters, not the cost.
- **36 questions (1997):** Pairs of strangers who took turns disclosing more and more over
  45 minutes felt closer than pairs doing small talk. Neither matched attitudes nor an explicit
  goal of getting close made a difference.
  [Aron et al. 1997, *PSPB*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167297234003)
  *Implication:* This is the pattern for Season 1. Order cards from light to deep, make
  disclosure turn-based and reciprocal (both answer before either sees), and let people skip.
  Do not copy the 36 questions verbatim. Write original questions that follow the same
  structure.

## 6. Gratitude

- **Find, remind and bind (Algoe 2012):** Gratitude points you to people who are responsive to
  you (find). It reminds you of good existing relationships (remind). It pulls you to invest in
  them (bind). [Algoe 2012, *SPPC*](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2012.00439.x)
- **Gordon et al. 2012:** People who appreciate their partners more are more responsive to the
  partner's needs, more committed, and more likely to still be together 9 months later. Feeling
  appreciated feeds appreciation back.
  [Gordon, Impett, Kogan, Oveis & Keltner 2012, *JPSP*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22642482/)
- **Evidence: good.** Multi-method, replicated findings.
- **Implication:** Season 4's main loop is gratitude. Make "one thing you did that I noticed"
  specific and tied to a trait ("…and it reminded me you're thoughtful"). Specific appreciation
  is what signals responsiveness. Keep it optional, and never prompt "you haven't thanked your
  partner in X days".

## 7. Capitalisation: responding to good news

- **Finding:** Telling someone good news and getting an enthusiastic, engaged response
  ("active-constructive") increases wellbeing and relationship satisfaction. Passive-constructive
  replies ("nice") are linked to *worse* relationship outcomes, as are the destructive
  types. How partners responded to good news predicted relationship outcomes, in some analyses
  more than responses to bad news.
  [Gable, Reis, Impett & Asher 2004, *JPSP*, summary](https://positivepsychology.com/active-constructive-communication/) ·
  [capitalisation in couples, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5407905/)
- **Evidence: good** (Gable 2004 and 2006 plus replications).
- **Implication:** Add a "good news" card type. Offer response chips that nudge toward
  active-constructive ("tell me everything", "how did it feel?") instead of a lone heart. It is
  cheap, positive, and fits every season.

## 8. Must-talk topics and cultural context

- **Premarital inventories:** In one study, PREPARE scores taken 3 months before marriage
  sorted couples who were happily married 3 years later from those who were divorced or
  separated with 80–90% accuracy. Topics included finances, children, family and friends,
  religion, roles, and communication. **Caveat:** The research is mostly by the tool's authors,
  and the samples are small (164 couples).
  [Fowers & Olson 1986, *JMFT*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1752-0606.1986.tb00673.x)
- **Relationship education in general** improves communication (d ≈ .43) more reliably than
  satisfaction. The satisfaction effects are modest and shrink once unpublished studies are
  counted. [Hawkins et al. 2008, meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18837590/)
- **Family approval matters:** The "Romeo and Juliet effect" did not replicate (N = 396).
  More interference and less approval from friends and family went with *worse* love,
  commitment and trust.
  [Sinclair, Hood & Wright 2014, *Social Psychology*](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1864-9335/a000181)
- **South Asian diaspora context:** Choosing a partner is often treated as a family decision.
  First- and second-generation children can disagree over arranged, assisted or self-chosen
  marriage. Gender-role conflict is linked to lower wellbeing in South Asian American women.
  Peer-reviewed work here is **thin and mostly US-based**.
  [Gordon lab, South Asian marriage attitudes (UTK)](http://web.utk.edu/~kgordon1/?page_id=307) ·
  [Insider view on cultural marriage norms, *Canadian Perspectives*](https://journals.library.ualberta.ca/cpi/index.php/cpi/article/download/24083/17890/59577)
- **Implication:** Season 4 (Commitment) should hold a gentle "big topics" pack: kids (if,
  when, how many), money (joint or separate, sending money home), faith and festivals, in-laws
  (living with parents? caring for them as they age?), where to live, careers and relocation,
  and roles at home. Ask about **both families' expectations** as a topic in its own right.
  Present it as "what we each imagine", never as a compatibility score. The research on
  premarital programmes shows the value is in *talking*, not in a verdict. Get native-speaker
  and community review, and avoid assuming every couple has involved parents, an arranged or
  love marriage, or a particular faith.

## 9. "Hard" vs "harmful": coercive control and abuse

- **Definitions:** The WHO defines intimate partner violence as physical, sexual or
  psychological harm, *including controlling behaviours*.
  [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/violence-against-women)
  Coercive control (Stark, 2007) is a *pattern* of isolation, surveillance, humiliation,
  micro-regulation and intimidation. It has been a crime in England and Wales since 2015
  (Serious Crime Act s.76).
  [Women's Aid: coercive control](https://womensaid.org.uk/information-support/what-is-domestic-abuse/coercive-control/) ·
  [CPS guidance](https://www.cps.gov.uk/prosecution-guidance/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-intimate-or-family-relationship)
- **Markers** (US National Domestic Violence Hotline): isolating you from family and friends,
  controlling money, checking your phone or location, humiliating you, threats, pressure about
  sex, jealousy framed as love, "you never do anything right".
  [The Hotline: warning signs](https://www.thehotline.org/identify-abuse/domestic-abuse-warning-signs/)
- **Not all conflict is abuse:** Johnson's typology separates violence rooted in control
  ("intimate terrorism") from situational couple violence. The key is the *pattern of control
  and fear*, not how loud the arguments are.
  [Johnson, Leone & Xu 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1077801214521324)
- **Couples work is usually contraindicated when there is abuse:** Joint formats can let
  control continue, silence the victim, and lead to retaliation at home.
  [Couple therapy and violence, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10813270/)
- **Tech abuse:** 97% of US domestic violence programmes report abusers using technology to
  stalk or control, including shared accounts and devices.
  [NNEDV Safety Net](https://nnedv.org/content/technology-safety/)
- **Implications (important):**
  - Season 2 copy must never say "every couple fights, push through" in a way that normalises
    fear. Include one line, shown privately: "Disagreeing is normal. Feeling afraid, watched or
    controlled is not."
  - Add a private, **single-person** "Is this OK?" page. It lists the markers and the
    country-aware footer already set in CLAUDE.md. It must never notify the partner or show
    up in any shared activity.
  - Do not build features that can be used for surveillance: no "last seen", no read receipts
    on private notes, no location, no "see what they saved". Either person can leave or erase
    at any time, and the app should not make leaving hard.
  - Season 1 (red flags) can include light "green flag / red flag" cards that are honest about
    control behaviours, never romanticising jealousy or possessiveness. This matters for Hindi
    film tropes as well.
  - Never keep people in a relationship by design. Success means clarity, and breaking up can
    be a good outcome.

For context, Mozilla's *Privacy Not Included* gave 22 of 25 dating apps its warning label.
Every romantic AI chatbot it reviewed also got one.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/23/mozilla-finds-that-most-dating-apps-are-not-great-guardians-of-user-data/)
None of the couples apps above clearly offers end-to-end encryption with an independent audit.
That is our gap.

**Copy:**
- Answer separately, then reveal (Paired, Agapé). This is the loop the category has proven.
- Credible, research-based decks that are free (Gottman Card Decks) and a quick start with no
  account.
- Shared anniversaries and memories (Between). Our 28-day room and on-device export is the
  honest version of this.
- Short, playful prompts over lessons.

**Avoid:**
- **Homework feel** (Lasting's courses, Evergreen's assessments and progress bars).
- **Points, streaks, progress scores** (Agapé points, Evergreen tracking). They conflict with
  our "no scores or guilt" rule and turn bids into obligations.
- **Paywalls just after onboarding** (Evergreen day 3, Paired). This is the top complaint. We
  are free.
- **Churn after novelty and repetitive content.** Our fix is seasons with a clear 28-day arc
  and end, not an endless daily feed. Ending well is a feature.
- **AI coaches reading couples' data** (Flamme, and Mozilla's chatbot findings). This is
  incompatible with our trust model.
- **Needing both people to be equally keen.** Keep solo actions (private notes, "guess their
  answer") so the less-keen partner is never nagged.
