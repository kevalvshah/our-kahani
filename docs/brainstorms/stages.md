# Stage brainstorms (five relationship stages)

Autonomous brainstorm, one session per stage, run with the product-brainstorming method
(frame → diverge → provoke → converge → capture). Inputs: CLAUDE.md, the five-stage model the
owner shared (Bader & Pearson / Campbell), the earlier Season 2 brainstorm, and everything already
built. Research references are collected in `docs/research/relationship-research.md`; the spec
that turns this into a build plan is `docs/SEASONS-SPEC.md`.

**Standing constraints for every stage.** The server can never read content, so anything that
"remembers" (Season 1 answers, patterns) happens on the couple's devices. No scores, streaks or
guilt. One tap or one line. Consent first for anything sensitive. Never label the couple with a
stage. Hard is ours to help with; harmful gets verified help and "leaving is not failing".

**Tone guardrail (owner, overrides everything below).** Our Kahani must feel playful and light,
something a couple *wants* to open, never heavy, clinical or vulnerable-by-default. So:
- Every season is mostly play: at least two playful cards for every reflective one, and a
  reflective card is always followed by a light one. Seasons read as fun first.
- The heavier tools (Dil ki Baat, the pause, "hard or harmful", Gentle Corner's deeper depths)
  are there when needed but never in the way: reached from a small "Talk it through" option,
  not from the home screen's main path, and never pushed by notifications.
- Language stays warm, desi and funny. No therapy words on screen ("trigger", "attachment",
  "conflict resolution", "vulnerability"). Say "when things feel a bit off", not "conflict".
- Reveals celebrate, tease gently and laugh; reflective reveals are warm, never analytical.
- Nothing is required: every reflective card has a pass, and skipping never shows anywhere.

**The job we are hired for (JTBD).** *When something between us feels hard to say or starting to
drift, I want an easy, kind way to say it and to feel close again, so we don't give up on each
other over something we could have worked through.* What people "fire" to hire us today: long
WhatsApp paragraphs, sulking, venting to friends or family instead of the partner, and doing
nothing until it is too late.

## Stage 1 · Infatuation (Season 1, Pehli Baat)

**Frame.** Chemistry is high and everyone shows their best self. The failure is not a fight; it
is *not asking*, so real differences (children, money, faith, where to live, how involved
families are) surface only after commitment. Diaspora twist: families are often already
involved, and "we'll figure it out" is common.

**Diverge.**
- Must-talk cards, framed as curiosity ("Honestly not sure yet" is always an option).
- "Green flags, amber flags" playful card: what you hope for vs what would worry you.
- A private "things to ask later" list (saved notes) so a question isn't lost in the moment.
- Bets on each other ("I bet you'd pick…") that reveal how well assumptions hold.
- 36-questions-style escalating self-disclosure, light to deeper across the season.
- "Family map": who matters, how often you'd see them, festivals, food at home.
- Reverse: *how would we make couples ignore red flags?* Keep everything shallow, make disagreeing
  feel like losing, reward sameness ("You both picked!" only). → So: celebrate *different* answers
  too ("Good to know 😄" already does this), ask the must-talks early, never score matches.

**Provoke.** Would must-talks kill the fun of a first season? Only if they read like an interview.
Placing them at the end of Season 1 (cards 15–18), after warmth is built, with a pass option,
keeps the playful tone. Also: do we risk pushing couples apart early? Better now than after a
wedding; the app should never judge the difference, only surface it kindly.

**Converge (owner's decision).** No must-talk cards: Season 1 stays playful and unchanged.
1. "What I learned about you" recap, built on the device from Season 1 answers both opened.
2. Keep "different answers" celebrated in reveal banners (already true).

**Riskiest assumption.** That the recap feels warm rather than like a report. Keep it short,
in your partner's words, and never compare or score.

**Integrates with.** Saved notes, Season 2's Then vs Now cards (which reuse the same answers),
the Our Kahani book.

**Parked.** Must-talk cards (owner: keep Season 1 light). Compatibility "report" (scores, forbidden). Family-member invites (privacy, scope).

## Stage 2 · Power struggle (Season 2, Asli Kahani) — the make-or-break

**Frame.** The illusion fades; habits irritate; old wounds get triggered; pursue–withdraw loops
start. Most relationships end here. The problem is rarely the difference itself; it is *how*
the couple talks about it (harsh start-ups, defensiveness, stonewalling) and whether repair works.

**Diverge.**
- "Say it softly" composer: *I felt… when… what would help is…* (feelings and needs chips,
  "when" about the moment, not the person). No "you always / you never" possible.
- One-tap replies: *I hear you / Can we talk tonight? / I need a little time, I'm not going
  anywhere / I'm sorry / Sending a hug*.
- "I need 20 minutes" pause: tells the partner "I'm overwhelmed, not leaving", with a timer and
  a banner on their screen; ends with "Ready to talk?".
- Repair shortcuts: *Sorry. Start again? Hug? I was wrong about something. Can we talk?*
- Then vs Now cards built from Season 1 answers ("You said chai. Still true?").
- "My stress, your support" cards: how each wants to be supported when stressed.
- Pursue/withdraw awareness cards, framed without blame ("When we disagree I tend to…").
- Differences we can live with: a card that names a difference and chooses "we'll live with
  it", "let's talk", or "let's try a small change".
- A "cooling" Antakshari round: a song each to lighten the mood after a talk.
- Reverse: *how would we make this stage worse?* Let people vent at each other in-app, show
  who "started it", notify the partner instantly with the full hurtful text, keep a score of
  apologies. → So: structured, blame-free messages; no score of anything; notifications say only
  "Your room needs attention"; nudge offline.

**Provoke.**
- *Wall, not bridge*: couples could fight through the app instead of talking. Every soft note
  ends with a gentle nudge to talk ("Talk about it over chai tonight?"), and replies are one tap,
  not threads. No back-and-forth chat.
- *Weaponising*: someone could use the composer to criticise. The structure (feeling + moment +
  need) makes blame hard to express, and "Take it back" removes it.
- *Harm*: if what's happening is control or fear, the composer is the wrong tool. A permanent,
  quiet "Is this hard, or is this harmful?" link sits on this screen.
- *Stonewalling as a feature?* The pause could be abused to avoid talking. The timer ends, and
  the banner invites "ready to talk?" — it's a break, not an exit.

**Converge.**
1. **Dil ki Baat** screen: Say it softly composer + one-tap replies + repair shortcuts.
2. **"I need 20 minutes" pause**, visible to the partner on every screen while it lasts.
3. **Season 2 cards** opening with 6 Then vs Now callbacks, then stress/support, repair,
   differences, boundaries, family expectations — every card has a pass.

**Riskiest assumption.** That a structured message reduces conflict rather than moving it into
the app. Test: in the first real couples, ask after a week whether they talked face to face
after a soft note (one-tap "Did you talk?" follow-up is a candidate).

**Integrates with.** Toasts ("Asha sent something softly" → opens Dil ki Baat), notifications
(generic line only), voice notes (say it instead of typing), Gentle Corner (deeper, planned
sharing vs in-the-moment Dil ki Baat), Saved notes (save what helps your partner).

**Parked.** AI rewording of messages (would require reading plaintext on a server — forbidden;
on-device models are too heavy for now). Therapist directory (later, with care).

## Stage 3 · Stability (Season 3, Sukoon)

**Frame.** The fighting settles; life gets comfortable and predictable. The risk is drift:
roommates who manage logistics. Research on novelty says shared *new* experiences restore
closeness; autonomy research says a secure couple can also have separate worlds.

**Diverge.**
- Novelty engine: Micro-Dates suggests ideas you haven't done yet; Movie Night offers a
  "something new for us" filter (a genre neither has watched).
- "Your world" cards: tell me about something you love that I don't do.
- A shared bucket list (dreams board light).
- "Surprise me" card: plan a tiny surprise within a week; reveal when done.
- New rituals: a weekly chai-and-check-in, Sunday walk, a song of the week (Antakshari).
- Story Relay chapters as an "alternate universe" version of us.
- Reverse: *how to make them roommates?* Only logistics, same routine, never new things, never
  curiosity about each other. → So: curiosity cards and novelty prompts, not planning tools.

**Provoke.** Is a novelty *engine* just another to-do list? Keep it to one suggestion at a time,
optional, never "you haven't done a date in 3 weeks".

**Converge.**
1. Season 3 cards on novelty, autonomy, rituals and small surprises.
2. Micro-Dates prefers ideas you haven't done (novelty without guilt).
3. The weekly huddle (shared with Stage 4) as the light ritual.

**Riskiest assumption.** That couples in a stable phase still open the app. Mitigation: the
toasts and the partner's small actions (a voice hug, a song) are the pull, not reminders.

**Integrates with.** Micro-Dates, Movie Night, Antakshari, Story Relay, photos (Right Now).

**Parked.** Location-based date suggestions (privacy and third-party data).

## Stage 4 · Commitment (Season 4, Saath)

**Frame.** A conscious choice to build a life. The risk is taking each other for granted as
responsibilities pile up (work, money, families, maybe children).

**Diverge.**
- **Shukriya jar**: one thank-you a week each; it opens when both have written (a small reveal).
- "I'd choose you again" card on an anniversary.
- **Weekly huddle** in three taps: best thing this week, hard thing, one thing I need.
- Money as a team, in-laws and families, sharing the load, life-admin + spark pairs.
- Capitalisation: "tell me your good news" cards where the reply is active and warm.
- Reverse: *how to make them take each other for granted?* Never say thanks, only talk logistics,
  let small resentments pile up unspoken. → So: gratitude ritual, huddle with a "one thing I
  need", and Dil ki Baat for the unspoken.

**Provoke.** A weekly ritual can become a chore. Both rituals are optional, never nagged, and
each takes under 20 seconds. The reveal (both wrote → both see) is the reward.

**Converge.**
1. **Shukriya jar** (weekly, revealed when both write).
2. **Weekly huddle** (three chips + optional line, revealed when both do it).
3. Season 4 cards: appreciation, choosing again, teamwork, money, families.

**Riskiest assumption.** That a weekly cadence fits real life. Mitigation: weeks with nothing are
simply skipped; nothing counts streaks.

**Integrates with.** Toasts ("Ravi filled this week's jar"), Saved notes (save a thank-you),
Time Capsule, voice notes (thank you in your own voice).

## Stage 5 · Co-creation (Season 5, Virasat)

**Frame.** A solid base, looking outward: family, work, giving back, travel. The risk is that the
romance becomes the least urgent thing.

**Diverge.**
- **Dreams board**: shared dreams by type (home, travel, family, career, giving back, learning,
  health, adventure); react and add.
- Traditions to keep or change (festivals, food, languages for children).
- Legacy cards: what do we want people to say about us?
- Romance anchors: every planning card paired with a spark card.
- **Our Kahani book**: a printable story put together on the device from answers, notes, and
  photos/voice notes still kept.
- Anniversary Time Capsule letters.

**Provoke.** Does a "legacy" season feel heavy? Keep it warm: dreams and traditions are joyful.
The book must stay on-device (the server can't read it), which also means it's private by nature.

**Converge.**
1. Season 5 cards (dreams, giving back, traditions, legacy, romance anchors).
2. **Dreams board** (shared, encrypted).
3. **Our Kahani book** (on-device, printable).

**Riskiest assumption.** That couples reach Season 5 in the app. Since everything is open from
the start, Season 5 also serves established couples who start with it.

## Across all stages

- **"What do we need right now?"** menu on Today: Spark / Talk it through / Just fun / Plan
  together / Say thanks. Routes to the right cards and tools whatever the season. This replaces
  any stage label.
- **"Is this hard, or is this harmful?"** page: plain signs of hard vs harmful, verified
  helplines (country-aware), "leaving is not failing". Linked from Dil ki Baat, Gentle Corner and
  Privacy.
- **Season memory on the device** (Then vs Now, What I learned, the book).
- **Always a bridge, not a wall**: soft notes and repairs nudge to talk in person.
- **Notifications stay generic**; toasts inside the app can name the partner (on-device only).

## Strongest direction

Stage 2 is the product's reason to exist: **Dil ki Baat (composer + one-tap replies + repair) and
the 20-minute pause**, together with Season 2's Then vs Now cards. Everything else supports it.

## Riskiest assumption overall

That in-app structure leads to *more* face-to-face conversation, not less. Build the nudge to talk
into every Dil ki Baat interaction and review it with the first real couples.
